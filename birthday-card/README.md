# A birthday card for Jayne 🎂

One file, no internet needed: `jayne-birthday-card.html`.
Open it in any browser and it plays — envelope, candles to blow out, wishes to turn,
six true things to tap, and a gift to open at the end. It works on an iPhone, an iPad,
an Android phone, a laptop, and on anything with a browser.

---

## Send it

**1 · A link is the smoothest way (best for an iPhone or iPad)**

Put the file online anywhere and email the link. It opens straight into Safari, full
animation, sound and all, with nothing for her to tap and hold. GitHub Pages, Netlify
Drop, or any web host you already use will do it — it is one static file, nothing to
install, no certificate, no database.

> Happy birthday! I made you something: **[link]**
> Tap it and turn your sound up. 🎂

**2 · Attach the file**

Attach `jayne-birthday-card.html` to the email. It is one file — no folder, no
dependencies.

- **Laptop or desktop:** she saves it and double-clicks. It opens in Safari, Chrome,
  Edge or Firefox and plays in full.
- **iPhone or iPad:** Mail shows the card in its attachment preview when she taps it.
  Some previews run the animation; some block the script — if hers does, the card still
  shows up as a clean, readable page with every word and her photo, so nothing is lost.
  If you want the full animated version on a phone or tablet, the link in option 1 is the
  dependable route.

**3 · Show it to her**

Open it on your own screen and tap through it together on a video call. Low tech, quite
lovely.

## Make it yours

Tap the ✏️ pencil at the top right of the card. You can change:

- her name, the occasion line, and the little line under the cake
- **the six wishes** (one per line — delete a line and the card quietly shrinks to fit)
- **the six truths** (one per line, written as `Title | the truth`, revealed when tapped)
- the message at the end, the sign-off, and who it is from
- the colour theme — Blush, Sage, Plum, or Night
- a photo of her for the last page (it is shrunk automatically so the file stays small)

Two things to know:

- Your edits are kept **in this browser only**. They never upload anywhere, and they do
  not travel inside the original file.
- So before you send it, tap **Save & download for email**. That saves a fresh copy with
  your words baked into it — *that* is the copy to attach or host.
- **Start over from the original** puts every word back. **Close the card** on the last
  page does the same and clears the edits from the device.

## What she will see

| Part | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| The envelope | Tap the wax seal — the note lifts out and the card opens |
| The cake | Five candles. Tap each one, or tap **Blow into your mic** and actually blow (where the browser allows a microphone) |
| The wishes | Swipe the card, or tap the arrows. Six wishes for the year ahead |
| The truths | Six tiles. Tap one and it turns over |
| The gift | Tap the ribbon. Confetti, a tune, and the message at the end |

Sound starts only after a tap, so nothing plays by surprise. The speaker button at the
top right turns it off. If a phone is set to reduce motion, the animations calm down.

---

## Why it will not break

- **One file, nothing external.** No fonts, images, scripts, or tracking from the
  internet. It works with the wifi off and nothing to load.
- **It degrades politely.** Some email apps and previews block scripts. In that case the
  card shows every word as a simple, readable page — including the words you edited in —
  rather than an empty screen. Nothing is ever hidden behind a script that did not run.
- **Phone first.** Sized for a phone and an iPad, in portrait and landscape, with the
  camera notch and the Safari toolbar accounted for. Tap targets are thumb-sized.
- **Private.** No analytics, no accounts, no uploads. Your edits live in your browser's
  own storage, and the downloadable copy carries them inside the file.

*Small print:* the card is a plain HTML file, so an email provider that refuses HTML
attachments (rare) will not send it — that is when option 2, a link, is the answer.
